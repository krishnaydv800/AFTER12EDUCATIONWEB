What is CAT?

CAT stands for Common Admission Test. It is a national level management entrance examination conducted for admission to postgraduate management programmes.

CAT scores are used by the Indian Institutes of Management (IIMs) and several other business schools as part of their admission process.

The examination mainly tests a student's quantitative ability, verbal ability, reading comprehension and logical reasoning skills.

Why Should You Take CAT?

  • Admission opportunity in IIMs.
  • Access to many reputed management institutes.
  • Strong career opportunities after MBA.
  • Development of analytical and problem-solving skills.
  • Opportunities in finance, marketing, consulting and technology management.
  • Useful qualification for students interested in business and management.

CAT Eligibility

Candidates should always check the latest official CAT notification because eligibility requirements can change.

Educational Qualification

  • Candidates generally need a bachelor's degree from a recognized university or institution.
  • Students appearing in the final year of their bachelor's degree may also be eligible according to the current notification.
  • Minimum marks/category requirements should be checked in the latest official notification.

Important

Completing Class 12 alone is generally not enough for CAT. CAT is primarily a postgraduate management entrance examination.

CAT Exam Structure

CAT is divided into different sections that test mathematical ability, reasoning, English language skills and reading comprehension.

Section Main Focus
VARC Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension
DILR Data Interpretation & Logical Reasoning
QA Quantitative Ability

The exact number of questions, duration, marking scheme and other details should be checked from the latest official CAT notification.

CAT Syllabus

1. Verbal Ability & Reading Comprehension

  • Tables
  • Graphs
  • Charts

  • Data comparison

3. Quantitative Ability

  • Arithmetic
  • Algebra
  • Geometry
  • Number System
  • Percentages
  • Profit and Loss
  • Ratio and Proportion
  • Time, Speed and Distance
  • Time and Work
  • Permutation and Combination
  • Probability

How to Prepare for CAT?

1. Understand the Syllabus

First understand the three major sections and identify your strong and weak areas.

2. Build Basic Concepts

Focus on arithmetic, algebra, reading comprehension, grammar, reasoning and data interpretation fundamentals.

3. Practice Questions

Solve questions regularly and analyse your mistakes. Simply solving many questions without analysing errors is not enough.

4. Improve Speed

CAT requires effective time management. Learn when to attempt a question and when to skip it.

5. Take Mock Tests

Mock tests help you understand your preparation level, improve time management and develop exam strategy.

6. Analyse Every Mock

After every mock test, check incorrect questions, unattempted questions and questions where too much time was spent.

What Happens After CAT?

After the CAT examination, institutes may use CAT scores along with other selection components.

Common Selection Components

  • CAT Score / Percentile
  • Academic Performance
  • Work Experience, where applicable
  • Written Assessment, where applicable
  • Personal Interview
  • Other institute-specific criteria

Every institute can have its own admission policy, so students should check the official admission information of each institute.

Common CAT Preparation Mistakes

  • Starting preparation without understanding the syllabus.
  • Ignoring Reading Comprehension practice.
  • Only focusing on Quantitative Ability.
  • Not practising DILR regularly.
  • Taking mocks without analysing them.
  • Spending too much time on one difficult question.
  • Ignoring revision.
  • Following too many resources at the same time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1. What does CAT stand for?

CAT stands for Common Admission Test.

Q2. Is CAT an undergraduate entrance exam?

No. CAT is primarily a postgraduate management entrance examination.

Q3. Can a Class 12 student directly appear for CAT?

Generally, no. CAT is intended for candidates who meet the required bachelor's degree eligibility.

Q4. Which subjects are tested in CAT?

The major areas are VARC, DILR and Quantitative Ability.

Q5. Is CAT useful only for IIMs?

No. CAT scores are also accepted by several other management institutes according to their admission policies.

Q6. Is CAT difficult?

CAT is competitive. Regular practice, strong concepts, mock tests and good time management can significantly improve preparation.
