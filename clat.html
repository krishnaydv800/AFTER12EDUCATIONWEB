What is CLAT?

CLAT stands for Common Law Admission Test. It is a national-level entrance examination mainly used for admission to undergraduate law programmes at participating National Law Universities (NLUs).

Students who have completed or are appearing in Class 12 can use the undergraduate CLAT route for admission to integrated law programmes, subject to the eligibility conditions mentioned in the current official notification.

Important: CLAT eligibility rules, examination pattern, participating institutions and admission requirements can change. Always check the latest official notification before applying.

Why Should You Take CLAT?

🏛️ Top Law Universities

CLAT can provide an admission route to several participating National Law Universities.

⚖️ Law Career

A law degree can open opportunities in litigation, corporate law, legal research, compliance and more.

📚 Integrated Programmes

Students can pursue five-year integrated undergraduate law programmes after Class 12.

🌐 National-Level Competition

CLAT provides a common entrance route for students competing for seats at participating institutions.

CLAT Eligibility

Educational Qualification

Candidates seeking admission through undergraduate CLAT generally need to have passed or be appearing in Class 12 or an equivalent qualifying examination.

Important Points

  • Students from different educational backgrounds can apply.
  • Students appearing for their qualifying examination may also be eligible, subject to the official rules.
  • Required marks and other conditions should be checked in the current CLAT notification.
  • Eligibility can differ according to the programme or institution.
  • Keep your Class 10 and Class 12 documents ready for the admission process.
Tip: Do not rely only on old eligibility information. Check the official CLAT notification for the admission year in which you are applying.

CLAT UG Exam Pattern

The undergraduate CLAT paper is designed to test reading, comprehension, reasoning, legal awareness and basic quantitative skills.

Section What It Tests
English Language Reading comprehension, vocabulary and language skills
Current Affairs & General Knowledge Current events and general awareness
Legal Reasoning Legal principles, facts and application of reasoning
Logical Reasoning Arguments, conclusions, assumptions and logical analysis
Quantitative Techniques Basic mathematical concepts, data and numerical reasoning

Note: The exact number of questions, marking scheme, duration and other examination details should be verified from the latest official CLAT notification.

How to Prepare for CLAT?

Understand the Exam

First understand every section, question style, syllabus and official instructions.

Improve Reading

Read newspapers, editorials and quality articles regularly to improve comprehension and awareness.

Build Current Affairs Knowledge

Maintain short notes of important national and international events.

Practice Reasoning

Solve logical and legal reasoning passages regularly.

Practice Mathematics

Revise basic Class 10-level mathematical concepts and practise data-based questions.

Take Mock Tests

Full-length mocks help improve speed, accuracy and time management.

Analyse Your Mistakes

After every mock, identify incorrect answers, guessing mistakes and time-consuming questions.

Daily CLAT Study Plan

Common CLAT Preparation Mistakes

  • Ignoring current affairs until the last few months.
  • Reading a lot but not practising questions.
  • Taking mocks without analysing mistakes.
  • Spending too much time on one difficult passage.
  • Ignoring quantitative techniques.
  • Preparing only from random sources without understanding the official syllabus and pattern.
  • Not maintaining revision notes.
  • Filling the application form without checking the official instructions.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is the full form of CLAT?

CLAT stands for Common Law Admission Test.

2. Can Class 12 students appear for CLAT?

Yes. Students appearing for or having completed their qualifying Class 12 examination may be eligible, subject to the current official eligibility rules.

3. Is CLAT only for students from Arts?

No. Students from different Class 12 streams can apply if they satisfy the eligibility requirements.

4. What subjects are tested in CLAT?

The major areas include English Language, Current Affairs including General Knowledge, Legal Reasoning, Logical Reasoning and Quantitative Techniques.

5. Is CLAT difficult?

CLAT is competitive. Strong reading ability, reasoning practice, current-affairs preparation and regular mocks can significantly improve performance.

6. Can I prepare for CLAT after Class 12?

The undergraduate admission route is intended for candidates meeting the applicable qualifying requirements. Check the current admission cycle rules.

7. Which colleges accept CLAT?

CLAT is used by participating National Law Universities and other participating institutions according to the applicable admission cycle.

8. What should I do after CLAT?

After the result, eligible candidates should follow the official counselling and admission process.
